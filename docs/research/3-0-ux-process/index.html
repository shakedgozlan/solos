How we make Sol's UX excellent

Good teams don't jump from a feature list to pretty screens. They settle the things, the places, the paths, the words and the structure, test it, and only then style it. Here is that process for Sol, and one choice for you.

The order good teams follow

  1. 3aThingsEvery noun in Sol (task, goal, entry, topic…) and how they link, so all apps share one model.
  2. 3bPlacesWhich screens exist and how you move between them.
  3. 3cPathsSix core flows, including empty, error and offline moments.
  4. 3dWordsReal Hebrew and English text and data before any layout.
  5. 3eStructureGrey screens: position, size and order only.
  6. 3fTestA week of real use on your phone, with a short voice diary.

Then UI with Atomic Design, using the grey structure as the skeleton. Why this order: object mapping (Sophia Prater), Shape Up breadboards (Basecamp), content-first layout, and small repeated tests (NN/g: three rounds of five beat one round of fifteen).

Keeping it from looking like AI made it

Designers say the problem with AI design isn't ugliness, it's sameness: the same gradients, fonts and three equal cards. What prevents it:

  • A short written design brief and a "never" list, followed on every screen.
  • Real content and every state designed, where generic work shows most.
  • Tiles sized by importance, and one signature element, likely the record button.
  • References picked by your taste from real apps (Mobbin), not the model's defaults.
  • A separate critic pass on every screen, so Claude never approves its own work.
